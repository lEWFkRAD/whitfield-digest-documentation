# Evidence binder — Whitfield County 2024 tax digest

**Prepared:** 2026-09-04  
**Status:** Public-record review; not an audit opinion or legal conclusion.  
**Audience:** Whitfield County residents, evidence reviewers, and any official evaluating a later referral.

## Executive finding

The reviewed records establish a genuine breakdown in the 2024 property-tax-digest process. Named officials publicly described political pressure over assessment levels and sharply uneven parcel effects. Tax Commissioner Danny Sane refused to sign the proposed digest. Courts intervened first to compel the ministerial signature and later to authorize temporary billing. The temporary basis used each parcel’s 2023 assessed value plus 10%, subject to explicit exceptions. Revised notices later replaced every June 2024 assessment. [R1][R6][P6][P8]

The financial consequences were real but must remain in separate categories. Commissioners reportedly approved $1.956 million for refunds. The FY2025 audit recorded two digest-related corrections at different reporting levels and found that temporary-versus-final differences were not reconciled timely. The same audit states that delayed digests created enough cash-flow concern for an unplanned, fully drawn $6 million TAN, repaid at year-end. [R5][P11]

The County’s General Fund capacity also fell materially: unassigned balance declined from $34,765,919 in FY2023 to $19,165,540 in FY2025, down $15,600,379 or 44.8726%. Total General Fund balance fell $12,192,725 or 34.5059%. The approved FY2026 budget then planned to use exactly $4,888,626 of fund balance. [P11][P12][C03–C05]

**Causal limit:** these facts establish reduced fiscal capacity and a specific digest-related liquidity event. They do not establish that the digest caused the entire reserve decline or planned FY2026 draw.

## 1. Institutional roles

- **Board of Tax Assessors / appraisal staff:** parcel valuation and digest preparation.
- **Tax Commissioner:** digest signature, billing, collection, refunds, and distribution; not parcel valuation.
- **Georgia DOR:** digest compliance review, ratios, millage and approval process.
- **Board of Equalization / appeal route:** assessment disputes.
- **Superior Court:** judicial continuity during the impasse.
- **County finance / Board of Commissioners:** budgets, transfers, financing, and fiscal management; not parcel valuation.

These functions overlap operationally but are not interchangeable. The presentation therefore avoids attributing all consequences to any one actor.

## 2. Chronology

### 2022–2023: rapid growth and an early collection dip

DOR’s county M&O export increased from $2,813,886,830 in 2022 to $3,497,424,806 in 2023: +$683,537,976 or +24.2916%. The county rate rolled down from 7.3 to 6.0 mills. FY2023’s in-year collection rate fell to 92.71%, compared with 100.00% in 2022 and 98.88% in 2021. The FY2023 audit remained unmodified and disclosed no material weakness. [P1][P2][P9][C01–C02]

This pre-failure collection dip is important because it shows that not every later weakness originated in the 2024 episode.

### March 2024: reported request for a limit

Gail Noles later stated that unnamed elected officials met the former assessor-board chairman and requested a set percentage increase. She said the board accepted the limits and compressing already-developed values caused “unexpected consequences.” Separately, Jevin Jensen said he and Dalton Mayor Annalee Harlan Sams met with assessors, and Jensen acknowledged suggesting “a small increase.” [R1]

The article does not prove Jensen and Sams were the unnamed March participants, provide an exact March date, or establish illegal intent.

### June–August 2024: refusal and mandamus

Reporting quotes Sane saying more than 800 commercial/industrial accounts fell by over $50,000 while more than 7,000 homes rose by over 50%. He refused to sign “in good conscience.” The assessor board petitioned for mandamus, and a visiting judge reportedly ordered him to perform the ministerial act of signing. [R1][R6]

The signed June letter, parcel analysis, and August 29 order remain missing. Reporting verifies the public dispute; those primary records are still required for final attribution and quantification.

### September–October 2024: review and temporary billing

DOR’s annual report confirms a 2024 performance review of Whitfield’s assessor board. The actual review file remains outstanding. [P4]

The temporary-collection order found that an impasse had prevented completion and approval and threatened government and school cash flow. Ordered paragraph 1 authorized the temporary 2023-plus-10% parcel basis with listed exceptions; paragraph 2 required millage setting and tax bills. Paragraph 4 allowed amended tax bills after DOR approval if necessary to tax each parcel correctly; it did not itself order refunds. [P6]

The order’s signature text reads October 7, 2024. A clerk-certified copy would provide definitive filing metadata.

### March 2025 onward: replacement and amended digest records

An official assessor release said revised values were produced March 11 and replacement notices would be issued for every June 2024 assessment, with a new appeal period. [P8]

DOR’s April 1, 2025 export reports a 2024 county M&O base of $3,736,104,129, up $238,679,323 or 6.8244% over 2023. Later DOR reporting lists a 4.300-mill county rate, 39.42 overall ratio, and 30.98 public-utility ratio deficiency with a $0 parcel penalty. [P3][P5][C10]

The exact DOR submission, rejection, resubmission, conditional-approval, and final-approval dates remain unavailable.

### September–December 2025: refunds and liquidity

Commissioners reportedly approved $1.956 million in unbudgeted refund funding; Jensen said roughly 9,000–10,000 taxpayers would receive refunds. [R5]

The FY2025 ACFR confirms that the County entered and fully drew a $6 million TAN because of delayed-digest cash-flow concerns. It repaid $6,050,250 principal and interest at year-end from 2024 property-tax receipts; issuance cost was $14,700. The $6 million principal is financing, not another loss. Direct financing cost was $64,950. [P11][C07]

## 3. Fiscal-capacity bridge

### Audited balances

- FY2023 unassigned / total General Fund balance: $34,765,919 / $35,335,153.
- FY2024: $29,462,476 / $30,022,486.
- FY2025: $19,165,540 / $23,142,428. [P11]

The FY2025 comparative schedule is used for consistent trend presentation. The original FY2023 balance sheet differs by $600; the later comparative presentation controls for this analysis.

### Interpretation of the “about $4 million” statement

The clarification established that the rounded phrase described reduced fiscal year draw, recurring operating expenses, and the FY2025 tax refund settlement; the county plans to rebuild reserves, but that rebuilding is not the purpose of the millage proposal.

That amount is a plan. It does not prove an actual FY2026 result, a loss, or a causal allocation to the digest.

### Annual fund flow

FY2024 General Fund revenue of $55,878,499 and expenditures of $61,328,112 produced a $5,449,613 deficiency of revenues under expenditures. Net other financing sources of $137,546 left a $5,312,067 net decrease. [P10][C08]

FY2025 revenue of $57,859,651 and expenditures of $71,645,575 produced a $13,785,924 deficiency. Net financing of $6,539,068—including $6 million of short-term debt—left a $7,246,856 net decrease. A separate $366,798 correction raised beginning fund balance. [P11][C09]

These statements demonstrate why the whole reserve decline cannot be labeled a digest loss.

## 4. Audit-control evidence

FY2024 finding 2024-001 addressed year-end-close controls and prior-period restatements. Separate findings covered segregation of duties, transaction voids, and deposit delays. [P10]

FY2025 finding 2025-001 specifically stated that temporary-versus-final digest differences were not identified and corrected timely, creating prior-period material misstatement. Note 22 separately decreased government-wide beginning net position by $294,076 and increased General Fund beginning fund balance by $366,798. These are different reporting levels and opposite directions; their absolute sum is not a published net correction or loss. [P11]

An unmodified opinion and a material weakness can coexist: the statements can be fairly presented after corrections even when the process that produced them was deficient.

## 5. Money-category reconciliation

- **$1.956M:** reported refund funding / budget authority; not automatically net loss. [R5]
- **$294,076:** audited government-wide beginning-net-position decrease. [P11]
- **$366,798:** audited General Fund beginning-balance increase. [P11]
- **$477,571.58:** derived countywide scale illustration; not parcel-level reconciliation. [C06]
- **$6M:** TAN principal borrowed and repaid; financing, not loss. [P11]
- **$64,950:** direct TAN interest plus issuance cost. [P11][C07]
- **$4,888,626:** approved FY2026 planned fund-balance use; fiscal-capacity measure, not digest-loss schedule. [P12]

A complete digest-specific financial result still requires a gross-to-net reconciliation of original bills, amended bills, refunds, credits, additions, receivables, unpaid amounts, collections, and financing costs.

## 6. Supported conclusions

1. The 2024 digest process materially broke down.
2. Elected-official pressure over assessment levels was publicly acknowledged, though the March participants remain unnamed.
3. Sane refused to sign based on stated valuation concerns and was reportedly compelled to sign.
4. Temporary court-authorized billing was required.
5. Every June 2024 assessment notice was later replaced.
6. The FY2025 audit linked material corrections to temporary-versus-final digest differences.
7. Refund funding and a documented TAN cash-flow event followed.
8. General Fund fiscal capacity fell sharply through FY2025.

## 7. Conclusions not supported by current records

1. Jensen and Sams were the unnamed March participants.
2. Any official committed fraud, a crime, or another specific illegality.
3. a named retailer or a specified development motivated the cap.
4. DOR reviewed “all assessments” or ordered elected officials out of appraisal decisions.
5. Temporary billing equaled parcel-level overbilling or cash refunds in every case.
6. The digest caused the entire General Fund decline or FY2026 planned draw.
7. TAN principal was a $6 million loss.
8. A complete gross-to-net digest loss has been calculated.

## 8b. September 2026 supplement (added 2026-09-10)

The 2026 millage fight produced three new record-backed facts, detailed in the wiki topic `whitfield-county-audit` (see `fy2026-budget-ocr-analysis.md` and `proposed-alternative-budget-2027.md` there):

1. **The county budgeted ≈5.49 mills in December and is asking 8.000 in September [C08; CL26/CL27].** The adopted FY2026 budget's property-tax line ($24,440,819, [P12] p.2, vision transcription cross-footing to $0) equals ≈5.49 mills on the audited FY2025 base — ≈$11.1M below the levy implied by the September proposal. The budget's own $32.8M revenue table names "Fund Balance" (+$4.9M) and "T.A.N. Proceeds" (+$4.4M) as the lines that grew to cover its $4.89M planned deficit; the 46-page adopted budget contains no mill number anywhere.
2. **The county formally acknowledged anticipated deficits [CL25; P13].** The TAN resolution on the Sept. 14, 2026 board packet ([P13] p.16) states, verbatim, "it is anticipated that deficits will be incurred by the County in cash flow during calendar year 2026" — a timing admission; always quote with "in cash flow" included — and authorizes $7,450,000 against a 75% constitutional authority of $20,504,069 (2025 gross collections $27,338,759), issued Sept. 16, due Dec. 31 at 3.48%. Borrowing inside the cap is lawful; the statement of anticipated deficits is the county's own words.
3. **Rate arithmetic reproduced [C08]:** $24,440,819 / $4,448,161,222 ≈ 5.494; FY2024 actual taxes ($19,154,483) on the same base ≈ 4.31 — last year's own rollback rate. The December board thus assumed the rollback rate *and* a planned reserve draw simultaneously.

Limits carried forward: [P12] p.2 figures are vision-transcribed (arithmetic ties; re-pull page images before quoting externally); the implied-millage method ignores exemption timing, districts, and collections lag; the county may amend the FY2026 budget at the Sept. 18/29 meetings — the watcher cron (`whitfield-millage-watcher`) checks for amendments and notices.

## 8c. Repair plan (added 2026-09-11)

Six management repairs, each anchored to a defect already classified in this repository, are in `repair-plan.md`: budget self-insured health claims at actual cost ([CL28], Note 19 verified against the ACFR text layer: $9.53M → $9.38M → $11.81M), a written fund-balance floor with a payback rule [CL16–CL18], a "rate required to balance" line in the budget resolution [CL26/CL27], a spending growth ceiling in planned-drawdown years [CL18], TAN size capped at documented collection lag [CL25], and mid-year rebalancing plus an independent variance report [CL13]. The plan is accounting discipline, not a service-cut proposal.
