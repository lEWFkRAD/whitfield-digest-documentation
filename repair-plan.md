# Repair plan — Whitfield County fiscal management (added 2026-09-11)

Six repairs, each anchored to a defect already in this repository's record set.
The plan does not say "vote the increase down." It says: book what you know, keep
the reserve you need, and put the purpose of any rate on the record. Nothing here
requires a fact this repository has not already classified PRIMARY, AUDITED, or
DERIVED.

## R1 — Budget employee health care at actual claims cost

**Defect on record.** The county self-insures employee health through an internal
service fund. Reported claim expense in the Group Health Benefits Self-Insurance
Fund: $9,527,244 (FY2023), $9,379,217 (FY2024), $11,806,770 (FY2025) — a 25.9%
one-year increase. [P11] p.109, Note 19 *Risk Management*, verified against the
ACFR text layer. Stop-loss sits at $100,000 per employee / $6,900,000 aggregate.

**Fix.** Budget the fund at run-rate plus a stated trend factor, and fund a
separate self-insured reserve so claims spikes hit a dedicated bucket instead of
the General Fund. A county that pays claims directly cannot budget claims by
hope.

## R2 — Written fund-balance policy with a floor and a payback rule

**Defect on record.** Unassigned General Fund balance fell $15,600,379 (44.87%)
FY2023→FY2025; total balance fell $12,192,725 (34.51%). [C03][C04], [P11] p.173.
No published floor or payback rule appears in any reviewed document.

**Fix.** Adopt a policy: a stated floor (GFOA practice for a rated county is
roughly two months of expenditures) plus a rule that any budget drawing below the
floor requires a written payback schedule the board votes on. This converts a
quiet reserve draw into a repayment plan on the record.

## R3 — The rate must equal the budget's own math, with the purpose stated

**Defect on record.** The adopted FY2026 budget's property-tax line
($24,440,819, [P12] p.2 **[V]**) implies ≈5.494 mills on the audited FY2025 base;
the September proposal is 8.000 mills ≈ $35.58M — an ≈$11.14M difference with no
stated purpose in any public document. [C10]; CL26/CL27.

**Fix.** Put a "rate required to balance this budget" line in the budget
resolution itself. If the county needs revenue beyond the budget line, it amends
the budget and names the purpose on the record — a transparent over-ask is legal
and survivable; a silent one is neither.

## R4 — Hold the growth line

**Defect on record.** The adopted FY2026 budget plans exactly $4,888,626 of fund
balance [CL18][P12], inside a $32.8M revenue table whose growth lines are
"Fund Balance" and "T.A.N. Proceeds" — reserves and borrowing are the revenue
source. [8b supplement]

**Fix.** A growth ceiling in policy (GFOA practice: General Fund spending growth
≤ revenue growth + 3% in a planned-drawdown year) plus a quarterly budget-vs-
actual variance report to the board.

## R5 — Borrow against receipts, not against holes

**Defect on record.** Three consecutive borrowing years: $6.0M TAN (2025,
unplanned) → $4.9M (FY2026 budget line) → $7.45M (Series 2026). The Sept. 16
resolution states, verbatim, "it is anticipated that deficits will be
incurred by the County **in cash flow** during calendar year 2026." [CL25][P13]
p.16.

**Fix.** Cap TAN size at documented collection lag, report revenue-vs-
expenditure monthly, and retire the practice of treating short-term borrowing as
a revenue line.

## R6 — Fix the plumbing that let this run three years

**Defect on record.** FY2025 audit finding 2025-001: temporary-versus-final
digest differences not reconciled timely; prior-year close issues recurred.
[CL13][P11] p.204. The adopted budget contains no millage figure anywhere.

**Fix.** (a) Rebalance the budget mid-year every year, publicly; (b) an
independent annual budget-vs-actual variance report; (c) no grant applications
approved with local funds silently backfilling before awards are known.

## What this plan costs the taxpayer

Less than the alternative. The repair that triggered the entire $11.1M question
is R1: three years of underbudgeting a known self-insured cost. The fix is
accounting discipline and one reserve, not a service cut.
